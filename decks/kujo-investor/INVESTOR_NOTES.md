## 01 — Trust at the
speed of AI.
Open with the accountability infrastructure thesis. The language creates long-term integration depth; tools offer a near-term adoption path. Financing scenario is illustrative.

## 02 — Software production is outpacing verification.
Use the illustrative completion claim, then explain that existing agents already log and test. The gap is assembling evidence across boundaries and deciding what is sufficient.

## 03 — Greater autonomy requires stronger accountability.
Walk left to right. Increased authority changes the consequences of failure. This is a conceptual progression, not a measured adoption forecast.

## 04 — AI use is broad. Trust remains conditional.
2026 usage question is multi-select with 17,464 respondents: 65.9% coding assistants or coding agents, 26.2% agents or automated workflows. Categories overlap and must not be added. The separate trust question reports 48% trust when answers are easy to validate. Self-selected survey; neither measure is autonomous production deployment. Source M03/M04.

## 05 — Build with clarity. Operate with control.
Clarity, Context and Control describe responsibilities. They are not one automatically enforced security perimeter. Expand the architecture details for exact component boundaries.

## 06 — Accountability spans the whole task.
Spec defines intent. PackWrite compiles the work pack. Dispatch orchestrates. Workcell applies host-dependent bounds. Eval checks outcomes. CaseFile captures output. RunLedger records supplied results. ShipCheck reports readiness; a human makes the release decision.

## 07 — The foundation is already being built.
Do not quote repository counts or test totals as demand. Distinguish released documentation from local execution. Workcell was source-reviewed, not container-certified during this session.

## 08 — A claim becomes inspectable evidence.
The demo is a deliberately seeded coding fixture. The agent corrected a post-money arithmetic bug. Show the original assertion failure, one-line diff, later successful command, and Eval artifacts. CaseFile records command output, not cryptographic proof of a trusted machine. No customer incident is implied.

## 09 — Adopt the ecosystem before the language.
Tools run in Kujo but can evaluate existing repositories and commands. Application code does not have to migrate. Workcell and Dispatch require environment-specific setup.

## 10 — Start with teams already delegating engineering work.
This is a proposed beachhead, inferred from technical need. Validate buyer ownership, investigation burden and willingness to pay with interviews and instrumented pilots.

## 11 — Open tooling can lead to a commercial operating layer.
The open tools exist. Hosted evidence, team service and enterprise packaging are proposals. Retention, custody, isolation and support need engineering before sale.

## 12 — A focused entry market can support a meaningful business.
Explain every assumption: 2,000 organizations times $24,000 annual spend = $48M; 100 customers = $2.4M annual revenue. These are arithmetic scenarios, not TAM research or forecasts. Expansion model is in appendix.

## 13 — Accountability requires more than generating code.
Acknowledge incumbent advantages: agent distribution, orchestration durability, observability and mature CI. Defensibility is a hypothesis: trusted evidence contracts, integrations and retained workflow usage, with no lock-in promise or superiority claim.

## 14 — Meet developers inside their existing workflows.
December 2026 is future execution. Track unique activated repositories, successful evidence runs, four-week retention, design-partner conversations and pilot conversion. Do not use stars alone as adoption.

## 15 — Execution is visible. Demand still needs proof.
Be explicit about unavailable data. This briefing cannot substitute for verified adoption and commercial diligence. Add independently confirmed usage and customer references before broad investor distribution.

## 16 — Built through sustained open-source engineering.
Use the first-person public biography cautiously. Do not claim every ecosystem commit is solely founder-written; collaborators and AI assistance must be credited. Security experience specifics require verification.

## 17 — Capital turns a technical foundation into an adopted platform.
$6M at $30M post-money implies 20% in the simplified model. SAFE conversion can differ due to options, other instruments and terms. Annual founder base is $150K plus modeled payroll burden. Budget includes reserve, not a promise of runway. Milestones measure adoption and revenue learning.

Close on responsibility. Kujo aims to make autonomous engineering accountable at scale. Offer the evidence appendix and contact page for technical follow-up.
