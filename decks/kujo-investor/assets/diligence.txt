# Commercial strategy — proposed, 2026-11-11

Decision recommendation: sell shared agent evidence and review to platform engineering leads at teams already operating coding agents across several repositories. This is a commercial hypothesis, not an available hosted SKU or demonstrated demand.

The purchase trigger is a consequential agent regression whose review requires reconstructing commands, revisions and failures across tools. Augment developer productivity/CI or incident-investigation budgets. Do not assume a compliance mandate or that logs are absent today. GitHub and LangGraph already retain useful history; interviews must establish an unmet operational need.

The first paid product must include repository/commit-bound evidence intake, searchable retained runs, team access controls, reviewer decisions, export/delete, retention settings, onboarding and documented support. Before accepting production customer data: tenant isolation, authentication, backup/recovery, retention/privacy terms and service ownership must be verified. Execution hosting is excluded from initial scope to limit cost and security surface. No active pilot or purchasable Kujo service was verified.

Open source is the entry path: a host adapter or MCP catalog leads to a first local failure/correction evidence run, then repeat weekly use, a budget-owner interview and a qualified design partner. Keep application languages unchanged. GitHub listing, releases and stars are distribution signals, not active users. Track consented cohorts rather than raw clones.

Primary price hypothesis: $500 per team per month, $6,000 annualized; a team workspace, not 1,000 individual developers paying premium subscriptions. This interpretation requires founder approval. Initial support/retention caps must be costed with design partners. Enterprise at $24,000/year is a separate deployment/support hypothesis, excluded from base revenue. The older 100 × $24K = $2.4M case is retained only as enterprise sensitivity; neither case is achieved traction.

ARR = retained paying teams × monthly price × 12. At $500/month, 17 teams = $102K ARR; 167 = $1.002M; 1,000 = $6M. First milestone: 12 buyer interviews, 3 qualified partners, 2 paid pilots, then 17 retained teams. These are gates, not promises. A proposed base trajectory reaches 17 by month 12 and 60 by month 24 ($360K ARR); $1M/$6M require later acquisition and retention proof.

Planning funnel: 100 qualified activations/month × 10% trial interest × 25% paid conversion = 2.5 gross adds/month once established. At 1.5% monthly logo churn, 60 teams lose 0.9/month; at 1,000, replace 15/month just to hold ARR. Reaching 1,000 requires a materially larger funnel, not extrapolating the starting one. Sales-cycle assumption: 1–3 months after activation; first paid revenue months 4–6. No pipeline currently substantiates these dates.

Assumed 75% gross margin implies $125 monthly COGS/team and $375 gross profit. At assumed $3,000 fully loaded CAC, payback is 8 months; simple gross-profit lifetime value at 1.5% monthly churn is $25K. These are diagnostic sensitivities, not measured unit economics; small cohorts cannot establish lifetime value. Cloud/model use, support time and refunds need invoices and workload data. Acquisition spend includes founder/customer-development time.

Retention hypothesis: weekly review of consequential runs, searchable history and usable exports. Measure time to first evidence run, four-week active teams, paid conversion, investigator effort and renewal. Interview assets are in customer-validation-plan.md.

Invalidation: fewer than 4 of 12 buyers can identify a recent costly reconstruction incident; no budget owner among 3 partners; no willingness to pay after demonstrated use; or support/compute exceeds $125/team/month without a justified price. Stop growth hiring, narrow to an integration/service engagement or evidence-export feature, and rerun discovery. Do not silently switch to enterprise revenue to rescue the base case.
